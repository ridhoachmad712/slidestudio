# Teaching patterns, depth, and revision

Read when selecting content depth, adapting across disciplines, or applying broad feedback. Use the lecturer's outcomes and slide function first; topic categories are aids, not rigid templates.

## Conversational brief

Accept a free-form request. Extract known audience, outcomes, sources, duration/count, function, expansion mode, and design. Summarize only material assumptions. Ask at most a few grouped questions that change the result, rather than presenting a long questionnaire. Offer depth examples from the available starter guide if abstract labels are unclear. Do not restart this process for every small revision.

## Concrete depth choices

- **Ringkas untuk ceramah:** include the main concept, essential assumptions, and evidence on slides. Place the longer explanation in notes.
- **Lengkap untuk dibaca:** retain the reasoning, definitions, and applied example in student-facing content. Split into coherent slides when allowed; notes cannot carry essential explanation.
- **Kasus dan latihan:** teach necessary concepts before using cases or problems. Include the information students need, lecturer prompts, and answer criteria. This is an approach that can be combined with either depth, not automatically a lower-content setting.

Depth must be observable: compare the resulting explanation and example, not just a label or word count. More text is not automatically more depth.

## Match explanation to content

| Content | Preserve |
|---|---|
| Concept/theory | Meaning, relation to other concepts, example and applicability limits |
| Calculation | Model/assumptions, notation, units, derivation steps, result and check |
| Decision | Alternatives, criteria, consequences, evidence and limits |
| Procedure/mechanism | Ordered steps, conditions/branches, likely mistakes and worked application |

Choose the relevant pattern per slide; mixed disciplines can use several. Do not add filler to complete a checklist. Calculations should not become only final formulas. Conceptual topics need not receive invented quantitative evidence. Editable diagram examples illustrate instructional relationships; scientific diagrams require verified content.

## Translate feedback into revisions

- “Lebih mendalam”: identify missing reasoning, conditions, contrasting cases, or interpretation. Add those within scope; do not merely lengthen wording.
- “Lebih mudah dipahami”: define unfamiliar terms, expose skipped steps, and add an appropriate example. Preserve precision and necessary caveats.
- “Lebih banyak kasus”: add situations that exercise the same outcomes and distinguish hypothetical examples from evidence.
- “Terlalu padat”: separate explanation and evidence or reorganize within the count. Ask only if essential content and mandatory limits cannot both fit.
- “Lebih kreatif”: use purposeful variation, available font pairings, and references. Keep the institutional template when mandatory.

Apply feedback consistently to matching parts of the deck while preserving unaffected material. For substantive revisions, check outcome coverage and assumptions again, render the revised output, and use a distinct version. Keep notes and any PDF consistent. Do not silently change scope, source expansion mode, or permanent profile preferences.

## Compatibility and user evidence

State which authoring, structural, render, and target-application checks actually ran. Local artifact-tool rendering is not PowerPoint, Google Slides, or a ChatGPT upload test. If fonts cannot be verified on the user's device, state the font requirement. Use available pilot feedback to improve defaults narrowly. An unperformed lecturer trial remains pending; never invent testimonials, success rates, or compatibility claims.
